# Diligence Flags

Lead with flags that change price, debt capacity, structure, or whether to proceed.

## Add-Back Quality

- Add-backs above 30% of reported EBITDA.
- Add-backs between 10% and 30% that need proof.
- "One-time" costs that appear in multiple periods.
- Run-rate, pro forma, or annualized adjustments inside LTM EBITDA.
- Cost savings from actions not yet completed.
- Adjustments normalized to an unstated benchmark.

Always compare headline adjusted EBITDA with supportable EBITDA: reported EBITDA plus only
supported add-backs.

## Forecast Support

- Forecast revenue growth materially above historical growth.
- Forecast EBITDA margin expansion with no named driver.
- First forecast year that does not reconcile cleanly to the LTM period.
- Price increases with no volume, churn, or elasticity discussion.
- Pipeline, backlog, or ARR used without definition or conversion support.

## Cash Conversion

- Working capital growing faster than revenue.
- No monthly or quarterly financials where seasonality matters.
- Capex without maintenance versus growth split.
- Capex below depreciation for several years.
- Adjusted EBITDA that converts poorly to free cash flow after capex, NWC, taxes, and interest.

## Concentration and Business Risk

- Customer concentration above 15%, or missing customer concentration disclosure.
- Supplier, product, geography, facility, license, or salesperson concentration.
- Contract renewal, churn, retention, or backlog metrics without definitions.
- Related-party transactions, litigation, environmental issues, or management departures.

## Missing Information

Track missing items separately from affirmative problems. Common missing items:

- Monthly or quarterly P&L.
- Revenue by customer.
- Churn, retention, ARR, backlog, or pipeline definitions.
- Pricing history.
- Maintenance versus growth capex.
- Working-capital detail.
- Debt-like items and transaction adjustments.
- Management compensation and rollover detail.

## Severity

- High: changes price, structure, debt capacity, or whether to proceed.
- Medium: changes a diligence workstream or a model assumption.
- Low: worth noting but unlikely to move the answer on its own.

Use High sparingly. A page where everything is High becomes less useful.
