# Public-data research

Design research around sector-specific, falsifiable diligence questions; never reuse a fixed industry list. Consider official administrative/regulatory records, courts/enforcement, loan/asset/property data, permits, procurement, inspections, complaints, labor, jobs, corporate registries, patents/trademarks, developer docs, certificate transparency, web/ATS changes, public filings, and industry-specific datasets. Prefer primary/official sources and record licensing/access constraints.

The workbook should contain row-level source data when available, URL, publisher, access date, grain, covered period, join keys, match method/confidence, derived fields, formulas, thresholds, limitations, checks, and reconciliation from raw records to headlines. Preserve raw extracts; transform separately. Govern entity matches and record exclusions.

Every quantitative statement in the brief must cite exact workbook tab/cell or range and the underlying source. Explain method, coverage, joins, limitations, and whether the conclusion is public evidence or inference. Never present illustrative totals as observed facts. For an explicitly requested demonstration, synthetic rows are allowed only when labeled throughout and modeled on the real schema.
