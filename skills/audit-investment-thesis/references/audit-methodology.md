# Audit methodology

## Criterion register

Build the register before evaluating the opportunity. Use one row per independently testable criterion.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Criterion | Testable proposition, not a broad slogan |
| Category | Situation, business quality, financial, customers/revenue, market, management, differentiation, deal dynamics, or other |
| Type | Mandatory gate, weighted preference, or supplemental |
| Definition | Exact metric, qualitative test, scope, and denominator |
| Threshold | Required, preferred, or tolerated range |
| Base weight | Weight implied by the current stated thesis |
| Behavior adjustment | Separately shown change supported by historical decisions |
| Final weight | Weight used in this audit; scored criteria total 100 |
| Exceptions | Conditions under which the firm has accepted a miss |
| Provenance | Source, date, location, and supporting historical cases |
| Confidence | High, medium, or low |

Split compound criteria. For example, do not score “durable, recurring growth” once; test growth level, durability, recurring revenue definition, retention, and underlying drivers separately.

## Common criteria to test

Use only criteria supported by the firm's mandate or clearly labeled preliminary inference. Common areas include:

- Situation: control, founder transition, carve-out, succession, complexity, proprietary access, geography, check size
- Business quality: mission criticality, retention, pricing power, cyclicality, capital intensity, working-capital demands
- Financial: revenue, EBITDA, growth, margin, cash conversion, leverage capacity, forecast quality, organic versus acquired mix
- Customers and revenue: recurring or contracted mix, retention, churn, customer concentration, cohort behavior, channel dependence
- Market: size, growth, fragmentation, end-market concentration, secular exposure, regulation, cyclicality
- Management: depth, succession, incentive alignment, gaps, operating readiness
- Differentiation: product or service advantage, switching costs, data, brand, network effects, intellectual property, route to market
- Deal dynamics: valuation, seller expectations, process, financing, add-on logic, ownership constraints

Keep deal economics separate if the stated thesis defines business fit independently of price. Add economics as its own criteria only when the mandate requires it.

## Weighting rules

1. Start with explicit weights when the firm supplies them.
2. Otherwise translate explicit priority language into a draft weight hierarchy and label it analyst-derived.
3. Adjust weights for demonstrated behavior only when multiple relevant decisions show a repeated, reasoned pattern. Cite those decisions and preserve the base weight.
4. Do not let the availability of data determine importance. A frequently reported metric is not automatically a heavily weighted criterion.
5. Keep supplemental criteria individually small enough that they cannot overwhelm mandatory or core business-quality criteria.
6. If weights cannot be responsibly assigned, use priority bands (`Critical`, `Core`, `Supporting`) and omit the numeric aggregate.

## Status and scoring

Assign each criterion a status before calculating:

| Status | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Fit | Evidence meets the criterion on the applicable definition and period |
| Exception | Evidence misses or strains the criterion, but an articulated exception may justify proceeding |
| Unresolved | Evidence is missing, conflicting, stale, or definitionally incomparable |
| Potential disqualifier | Evidence indicates a mandatory gate may fail or a severe risk exists |
| Not applicable | Criterion genuinely does not apply to this opportunity |

When a numeric score is useful, use a transparent 0–4 scale:

- `4`: clearly exceeds the preferred standard
- `3`: meets the criterion
- `2`: borderline or supportable exception
- `1`: materially below preference, with a narrow path to acceptance
- `0`: confirmed failure

Do not assign a numeric value to `Unresolved` or `Not applicable`. A `Potential disqualifier` can be scored only if the underlying failure is confirmed; otherwise keep it unscored and prominent.

For scored criteria:

`weighted points = final weight × score / 4`

Report both:

`evidence-covered score = sum(weighted points) / sum(scored weights) × 100`

`evidence coverage = sum(scored weights) / sum(applicable weights) × 100`

This prevents unknowns from appearing as either failure or neutral performance. Label the aggregate `Provisional` when evidence coverage is below 70%, when a mandatory gate is unresolved, or when material source conflicts remain. Use a different coverage threshold only when the firm supplies one.

Do not invent a pass score. If the firm has no calibrated threshold, use the aggregate to compare criteria, not to declare approval.

## Exceptions and disqualifiers

For every exception, state:

- The exact miss and magnitude
- Whether the exception is allowed by stated criteria or only observed historically
- Comparable accepted and rejected cases
- The compensating strength or mitigation required
- The owner, evidence, and deadline needed to validate it

For every potential disqualifier, state:

- The mandatory gate or severe risk implicated
- Whether failure is confirmed, indicated, or merely possible
- The source and confidence
- The shortest diligence step that could confirm or clear it

A confirmed mandatory failure overrides the aggregate score. Never downgrade one into a routine exception merely because the opportunity scores well elsewhere.

## Historical analogues

Select analogues for the question they illuminate, not superficial company similarity. Use a compact comparison across target, relevant facts at the original decision date, outcome, rationale, similarity, difference, and implication.

Avoid hindsight leakage. Compare using facts known at the stage corresponding to the current opportunity when possible. Distinguish:

- Invested and performed well
- Invested and underperformed
- Passed for thesis reasons
- Passed for price, process, timing, or capacity
- Lost or withdrawn without a thesis conclusion

Do not present portfolio performance as causal proof that a criterion is valid.

## Diligence prioritization

Rank open questions by `decision impact × uncertainty × ease of resolution`. Ask questions that can change a gate, status, or high-weight criterion first. Each question should identify:

- The current claim or unknown
- The criterion and weight affected
- The exact data, definition, cohort, reconciliation, or document needed
- The conclusion that would change under favorable and unfavorable answers
