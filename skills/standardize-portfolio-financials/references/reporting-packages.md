# Reporting packages

Build only the package requested by the user. Treat a supplied sponsor, fund, or LP template as authoritative. Preserve its branding, page order, metric definitions, disclosure language, and level of detail unless the user requests a redesign.

## Internal-review package

Include, when supported by the source materials:

- Executive portfolio summary and reporting-scope status
- Company-level actual, budget, forecast, prior-year, and LTM performance
- Revenue, gross profit, EBITDA, margin, cash, debt, liquidity, covenant, and operating KPI views
- Reported-to-standardized and reported-to-pro-forma bridges
- Portfolio aggregation and company contribution analysis
- Material variances and source-grounded commentary
- Data-quality, mapping, reconciliation, and approval exceptions
- Detailed lineage or a linked supporting appendix

Expose unresolved issues prominently. Internal packages may include review notes and mapping details, but keep them visually distinct from publishable financial results.

## LP-facing package

Include only externally authorized information. A typical structure is:

1. Cover, fund or portfolio name, and reporting period
2. Basis of presentation and confidentiality notice
3. Portfolio overview and highlights
4. Portfolio financial and operating summary
5. One consistent section per portfolio company
6. Definitions, footnotes, and required disclosures

For each company, use a consistent hierarchy: concise company context, period performance, selected KPIs, liquidity or leverage where authorized, key developments supported by source materials, and clearly labeled outlook when supplied.

Exclude internal mapping confidence, unresolved analyst commentary, privileged deliberations, unapproved adjustments, raw exception logs, and sensitive company details that are not authorized for LP distribution. Do not turn an internal-review artifact into an LP package merely by changing its title.

## Presentation rules

- State currency, units, period, cadence, actual or forecast status, and reported or pro forma basis on every relevant view.
- Keep reported, standardized, and pro forma values visually distinct.
- Use consistent company ordering, metric definitions, decimal precision, chart scales, and favorable or unfavorable variance conventions.
- Show `N/A`, `not provided`, or `not tested` instead of zero for unavailable data.
- Use tables for exact values and charts only when they improve comparison or trend comprehension.
- Keep footnotes near the affected metric and carry adjustment and definition disclosures through every relevant page.
- Label preliminary, unaudited, management-provided, proposed, or review-required results accurately.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives, causal claims, and forward-looking statements.

## Delivery controls

- Produce an editable source file and a rendered PDF when the requested tools and format support both.
- Verify page count, page order, table of contents, headers, footers, dates, company names, confidentiality markings, and page numbers.
- Confirm that charts, footnotes, and tables agree to the final standardized workbook after recalculation.
- Check for clipping, overlap, low contrast, broken fonts, blank pages, stale template text, comments, speaker notes, hidden slides, and internal-only metadata.
- Keep a separate internal exception report even when the outward-facing package is clean.
- Do not describe a package as final or distribution-ready while any critical control affects its published results.
